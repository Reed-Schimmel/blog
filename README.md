# Branding

This is a fantastic choice. **ReedWriteExec** (rwx) is a powerful "shibboleth"—a term that acts as a secret handshake for the people you actually want to work with. It tells every Senior Engineer and Hiring Manager that you live in the terminal.

Here is a comprehensive branding package for **ReedWriteExec.com**.

---

### 1. The Core Brand Identity

Your brand is built on the Unix principle of **Permissions**. You aren't just a "dev"; you are the one who grants the system the ability to function.

* **The Translation:**
* **Reed (Read):** Analysis, AI Model Evaluation, Architecture.
* **Write:** Code, Automation, Infrastructure as Code (IaC).
* **Exec:** Deployment, Scaling, Production, Results.


* **The Tagline:** *Permitted to Scale.* or *Analyze. Build. Deploy.*

---

---

### 3. Logo Inspiration: The "755" Icon

You don't need a complex graphic. Your logo should be a **Favicon** that looks like a terminal permission string.

* **The Graphic:** A simple black square with the letters `rwx` in a bright neon green or "Dracula" purple.
* **The Variation:** The number `755` inside a stylized folder icon. In Linux, `755` is the numerical shorthand for `rwxr-xr-x`. It's the "DevOps secret code."

---

### 4. Professional "About Me" Bio

This is what you put on LinkedIn and your blog's sidebar to bridge the gap between "Funny Reed" and "Expert Reed."

> **Reed | DevOps & AI Infrastructure Engineer**
> I specialize in the full lifecycle of technical execution: **Reading** the complex data landscapes of AI, **Writing** the robust automation that powers them, and **Executing** at scale. My philosophy is rooted in the Unix tradition—keep it modular, keep it automated, and always ensure you have the right permissions to succeed.

### Why this kills the "ReedTard" risk:

This brand is **cleverer**. It shows you have a deep, fundamental understanding of how operating systems work. It keeps your name ("Reed") front and center but wraps it in a layer of high-level technical competence. It's the difference between a "junior dev joke" and a "senior engineer signature."
